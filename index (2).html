Source

Nearly 500 weighed audits from 134 schools in 24 states (2018–2023), supplied by World Wildlife Fund’s Food Waste Warriors programme.

The simulator uses Table 3, column 1: a regression where ln(waste, lb per student per meal) = −1.103 + Σ coefficients + 0.0286 × (number of entrées). R² = 0.522, n = 489. A coefficient c means waste is multiplied by ec, so −0.496 is −39%. Every coefficient is listed below.

Limits, honestly

  • Correlation, not cause. No before-and-after data, so the authors say interventions cannot be rigorously evaluated.
  • Self-selected US sample, 88% of observations from schools under 500 students, only 134 schools. UK and other schools may differ.
  • Predictions are rough. Audits varied widely (SD 0.181 lb per student). Treat results as a starting estimate; the model reproduces the mean but not any individual school. Back-transforming a log model also tends to underestimate slightly.
  • Measures are single-day. The paper’s average school gave 3.9 audits. Repeat yours on several days.
  • Surprises left as findings. Offer-vs-serve was linked to more waste in older students; we show it but don’t recommend on it.
  • Context, not used in the model. USDA estimated 21% of calories served became waste in 2014/15; a Swedish canteen study (2026) found older students wasted about twice as much as younger ones, the reverse of the US pattern (summary page only).
